Craggenrock tidbits

Things I could've shared but didn't

Here are some little things about Craggenrock some people might find interest in. Most of these are stuff I really could've posted as tiny tidbits for marketing, but well... this site exists so... 🤷.

How did Craggenrock's development start?

Craggenrock actually started as an idea for an RPG similar to Summon Knight: Swordcraft Story around 2018. Early on, I've already wanted to create something that heavily highlighted crafting. I planned it to be a bit more serious and stuff, but soon realized I was over-scoping my (first) game by a huge mile.

Multiple revisions years after, I "scaled" it down to focus on just the crafting because that's what I really wanted at the time. (I think I may have even stripped too much...)

If you want a more in-depth rationale behind Craggenrock and the crafting, I've already published a writeup on it years ago on Steam.

It's really just crag and rock = Craggenrock. Everybody's so creative, amirite?

What was Craggenrock the game before it got its final title?

For the longest time, it was just Project Crafter to me. At some point, I wanted to reference the word "meteor" so it became Ameteos briefly. There was Mallic too, related to malleus or hammer/mallet in Latin. Those names looked like they were trying too hard to be unique, so I dropped them.

How did you come up with the character names and locations?

Important NPCs are supposed to be allusions. Arlo, Luna and Hannah are exceptions.

  • Arlo - apparently means hill. Afaik, this was unintentional. It just seems like a name that's "soft" on the tongue and it sounded cute, so I went with it.
  • Luna - moon in Spanish. She has always been meant to be named like this. No special reason.
  • Braso - Spanish for arm. He's a reliable member of the town, a shoulder you can rely on.
  • Isalin - means to translate/transfer in Filipino. She moves around a lot, never in one place.
  • Karron - based on the Hiligaynon term for later, karon. Things you buy from him arrive later than immediately.
  • Pieza - Spanish for piece. She's named like this for a specific reason I cannot say because of possible spoilers.
  • Hannah - just a name I thought of, the particular reason of which I cannot share due to, again, possible spoilers. :)
  • Mano - Spanish for hand. It's more of a reference to the Filipino gesture of respect, pagmamano.

Adventurer names are just a collection of Spanish, Italian and Filipino names I could think of.

For the locations:

  • Pareho - Filipino for same/similar. It's the town that will never be the same after the disaster. I think it's some sort of poetic self-contradiction in one way or another.
  • Tikas - Filipino for posture/poise. It's usually associated with muscular/fit builds, which are what I imagine healthy miners should be.
  • Halina - Filipino for "come along", an invitation. Similar to Pareho, it's meant to have that self-contradiction, seeing the island is infested with monster.
  • Tarong - Hiligaynon for proper. 
  • Sibucao - apparently a type of plant. There are some real places named like this.
  • Imbis - Filipino for instead.
  • Three Cheers Tavern - This one has 2 intentions behind it.
    1. Three cheers usually refers to the "hip hip hooray" cheer. There's a popular segment in an old noon-time show that did this as a game. It's really frantic and fun.
    2. The three cheers are supposed to refer to the times adventurers/mercenaries will have a toast: one before heading out hoping things will go right, one during your escapade to drown out the stresses and one after you've come back, still alive. Some of them will probably only get to do the first 2. A bit somber, yeah. 😔

I use a lot of Spanish, Filipino and Hiligaynon references in the game due to my background. (I'm Filipino, Spanish is quite common in the Filipino language and I have lived in Iloilo for around a decade, making me familiar with Hiligaynon.) You'll see more examples in the following sections.

How did you name the materials?

I wanted to break away from using the typical material progression (wood -> bronze -> silver, etc.), but also needed something that can hint what they're supposed to be, so I made all major materials follow a certain naming convention.

Ores

Ores are named after real life mountains/volcanoes + a suffix. The suffices indicate if they are a basic ore or an "alloy" as I would call them (ores that can result from combining other ores).

-on for the "alloys", -ite for the basic ones.

  • Marsilon - Marsili + -on
  • Tagron - Tagoro + -on
  • Malton - West Mata + -on

  • Saxanite - Saxani + -ite
  • Linzorite - Linzor + -ite
  • Elgonite - Elgon + -ite
  • Wistrite - Wister + ite
  • Galatite - Galatea + -ite

Craggenrock and Sunstone are unique, so both of them end with ground-related suffices (-rock and -stone). [Fun fact: Craggenrock the material used to be named Fragstone before!]

Wood
e.g. Navaro, Eder, Foster 

All wood are based on last names. I liked the idea of family trees. Arboris is different because it comes from arbor.

Yarn and Cloth
e.g. Madrigal, Falsetto, Medley

They are named after musical terms. I thought both music and cloth can flow.

Leather
e.g. Oroya, Sabal, Ensete

They used to be named after actual leather names, but I've since updated them. They're genus names for plants afaik.

Hides
e.g. Downy Hide, Porous Hide, Slippery Hide

I'll be honest, I kinda gave up on these lol. I just took whatever adjectives would work to describe rawhide.

Other Materials

I simply followed an adjective + noun pattern for most of them, being extremely careful not to use any terms that denote color or those that imply a certain level of quality. This is how we ended up with the likes of Cute Feather, Fragrant Herb and Thick Sap. Not very exciting, yes. A few of them are portmanteaus. (e.g. Snaptalon and Gastracid)

Food

They're mangled references to real Filipino food.

  • Binitin - based on puto bumbong, but the name is derived from a different food called inipit.
  • Humuk - based on the plain puto. The name itself comes from the Hiligaynon word humok, which means fluffy/soft.
  • Karin-karin - based on carioca. The name is supposed to reminiscent of a different meal, kare-kare.
  • Gataan - based on ginataang bilo-bilo. I just took the gataan part.
  • Bucao - based on the Hiligaynon term bukaw, which afaik means big-eyed. There's an actual fish that fits this description called bukaw-bukaw in IIoilo.
  • Panabik - based on palabok. Panabik is a result of that mangling.
  • Rojas - based on the hotdog on a stick, the one with the marshmallow at the end. People usually associate that as a party staple in Filipino kid's birthday parties. Roja is just derived from rojo, Spanish of red. 'Coz we got red hotdogs here. (Yes, I know)
  • Binilog - based on the pianono bread. Binilog means "made round" in Filipino. Pianono afaik has that spiral pattern 'coz it's just sheet cake topped with cream, rolled then slice, explaining the spiral.
  • Bonanza - based on the banana que (or cue?). Bonanza is close enough to banana.
  • Subanon - based on chicken inasal. The Hiligaynon term for grilled stuff is "sinugba". Subanon is derived from that.

  • Tanso - based on coffee. Tanso is the Filipino term for copper. Coffee is dark brown-ish, copper is brownish. You get the idea.
  • Lagundi - is actually just the name of the plant and the drink derived from it. It's known for cough relief. (I think this one slipped the cracks...)
  • Darin - based on orange juice. I love citrus, especially mandarins 🤷
  • Maras - based on mango juice. There's a special variety of mangoes here that comes from the island of Guimaras.
  • Ginkurog - based on halo-halo. It's derived from ginakurog, Hiligaynon term for shivering.

These ones, I don't remember the name references to.

  • Sirado - based on kare-kare.
  • Sarian - based on the tinola.
  • Tatampo - based on the buko pandan.
